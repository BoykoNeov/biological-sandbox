4b — the drawing path

Two renderers, both consuming protocol data only: traces — stochastic replicates overlaid on the deterministic limit they collapse into — and fields, straight from fields() through a numpy colormap to putImageData.

The slice measured what drawing costs the CPU and could not measure whether the result is smooth: putImageData runs whether or not the tab is visible, but compositing and vsync do not. It carried a probe that waited for a visible tab rather than faking one. This is that probe.

Conditions

1. Traces — the project's organizing picture

Four stochastic replicates of the repressilator, streamed while they run, over the RK4 solution of the mass-action ODE they converge to. The limit is drawn thick, the replicates thin and translucent; both come from the same model through the same protocol.

Two panels, by the model's own grouping. Six oscillators in one frame cross each other constantly and no single curve can be followed. The split is mRNA and protein, and each gene keeps its hue across both, so gene 1's mRNA and gene 1's protein are the same colour in the two panels.

The two panels come out looking almost the same, and that is a result rather than a duplication: at β = 1 the protein and mRNA timescales are equal, so each protein simply follows its own mRNA with a lag. Their ranges are also comparable — the limit runs to 153.0 in mRNA and 135.4 in protein — so this is not a case of one series being flattened by another's scale. (It was written up as one here, from a guess, until the numbers were checked.)

2. Fields — and what a finer grid really costs

Gray-Scott at Pearson's parameters, swept over three grid sizes. dt is derived from the grid rather than typed: the CFL limit forces it down as the grid refines, so a step size that is stable at 64² is unstable at 256², and quoting frames per second hides that a finer grid pays twice.

This pattern is exploratory. At Pearson's (F, k) there is no real non-trivial homogeneous steady state, so these are not Turing patterns and linear stability analysis makes no claim about their wavelength. Nothing here is validated; the validated Gray-Scott claim is the dispersion relation, and it is a scalar.

What that square is, so it is not mistaken for a result: this section runs 400 steps per grid, which is t = 200 / 80 / 20 — so the picture is the seeded initial condition, barely moved. Pattern formation at these parameters is t ~ 10³, tens of seconds of continuous stepping. Gray-Scott does not make squares; the initial condition is a square patch, and this panel is a stopwatch rather than a demonstration. The running pattern is in the demo.
